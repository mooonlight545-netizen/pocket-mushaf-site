Why a device

Phones make reading fragile.
This makes it inevitable.

Every phone mushaf lives three taps from a feed. This device holds one book. Open it and you are where you left off, on a paper-like screen that reads beautifully in sunlight.

Reading, the way the page was designed

Three, four, or five mushaf lines per view — each line laid out by the mushaf's own layout data, scaled to fill the screen edge to edge. A quiet progress line tells you where you are: 13% · Juz 5 · An-Nisa.

  • Jump to any surah, ayah, juz, or page in a few presses
  • Bookmarks that cycle with a single button
  • Surah headers drawn with the mushaf's own ornament art

Every detail from the print

Surah bands with rosette ends, the basmallah in its authentic calligraphy, centered closing lines — because a mushaf is not just text, it is a page you know by heart.

The hardware

Five buttons. One job.

Four arrows on the front: left and right turn the page, up enters hifz mode, down drives the drill. The power button sits on the right edge where your thumb rests — press to jump anywhere, hold for sleep. USB-C on the left edge is the only port: charging and firmware updates, nothing else.

Hifz mode

Hide the lines.
Recite. Reveal.

One button hides the screen's lines behind quiet dashes. Recite from memory, then reveal line by line to check yourself — or toggle a single stubborn line you are drilling. Your memorization state is saved on the device, expressed in ayahs, portable across every future version.

Updates

New firmware, no network

The device never touches the internet — that is the whole point. Updating takes a USB-C cable and a browser.

  1. Plug inConnect the device to any computer with the USB-C cable.
  2. Open the flash pageIn Chrome or Edge, visit the flash page on this site.
  3. Click installYour reading position, bookmarks, and hifz state are untouched — they live on their own storage, by design.
